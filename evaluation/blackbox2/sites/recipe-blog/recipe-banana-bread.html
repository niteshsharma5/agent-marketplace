Banana Bread That Actually Stays Moist

By Marge Callahan · February 18, 2026 · Breads, Breakfast

There is a specific kind of guilt that comes from throwing away spotty bananas, and I think every home baker knows it well. My grandmother never let a banana go to waste in her life. She kept a little bowl on top of the fridge specifically for bananas that had gone "past eating but not past baking," and that bowl was the beginning of more loaves of banana bread than I could possibly count growing up.

For years, my own banana bread came out dry by the second day. It was fine warm from the oven, smothered in butter, but by the next morning it had turned into something closer to a brick. I tried adding more banana. I tried adding yogurt. I tried baking it at a lower temperature for longer. Nothing quite fixed it until I finally sat down and really thought through what was happening chemically, rather than just throwing more ingredients at the problem.

The answer, it turns out, was brown butter and buttermilk, used together. Browning the butter first adds a nutty, caramel depth that plain melted butter can't match, and it also removes some of the water content that would otherwise evaporate and dry out the crumb during baking. The buttermilk, meanwhile, keeps everything tender by weakening the gluten structure just enough that the bread doesn't turn tough as it bakes.

The second discovery was about the bananas themselves. Most recipes call for "ripe" bananas, but I've found that truly overripe bananas — the ones that are almost entirely black, that most people would throw straight in the trash — give you a far more intense banana flavor and a wetter batter that bakes up incredibly moist. Don't be afraid of bananas that look too far gone. For this recipe, "too far gone" is exactly where you want them.

This loaf has become the thing I'm known for at every work potluck and school bake sale. I make a double batch every couple of weeks now, one loaf for the freezer and one that never makes it that far. My oldest likes to eat the heel of the loaf standing up at the counter before it's even fully cooled, which used to drive me up the wall until I remembered doing exactly the same thing in my grandmother's kitchen thirty years ago.

Banana Bread

Prep time: 15 minutes Bake time: 60 minutes Yield: 1 loaf (10 slices)

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, browned and slightly cooled
  • 3 very ripe (mostly black) bananas, mashed
  • 3/4 cup brown sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/3 cup buttermilk
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 3/4 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5-inch loaf pan.

  1. Whisk the browned butter, mashed bananas, and brown sugar together until smooth.

  1. Beat in the eggs, buttermilk, and vanilla extract.

  1. Whisk together the flour, baking soda, and salt, then fold gently into the wet ingredients until just combined.
  2. Pour into the prepared pan and bake for 55-65 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean.
  3. Cool in the pan for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a wire rack to cool completely before slicing.
