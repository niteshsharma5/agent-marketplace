Faculty

Dr. Miriam Halvorsen

Dr. Halvorsen is a Professor in the department whose research covers distributed systems, fault tolerance, and large-scale storage infrastructure, and she has led several externally funded projects in these areas over the past decade in collaboration with both government agencies and private industry partners, publishing widely in the top systems venues and serving on multiple program committees; she joined the department in 2003 after completing her doctoral work and has since supervised more than a dozen graduate students to completion while also teaching the department's core systems and networking courses at both the undergraduate and graduate level every year without interruption.

Dr. Andres Okafor

Dr. Okafor is an Associate Professor specializing in artificial intelligence and machine learning, with particular emphasis on reinforcement learning methods applied to robotics and autonomous decision making, and his lab regularly hosts undergraduate research assistants alongside graduate students, funded in part through a combination of federal grants and industry gifts; prior to joining the department he was a postdoctoral researcher at a national laboratory, and he currently teaches the department's introductory and advanced artificial intelligence courses as well as a graduate seminar on sequential decision making each spring.

Dr. Priya Nadarajah

Dr. Nadarajah is an Assistant Professor whose work sits at the intersection of human-computer interaction and accessibility research, focusing on how assistive technologies can be designed more effectively for users with visual and motor impairments, and she runs a small research group that partners with local community organizations to conduct field studies outside the traditional laboratory setting; she also coordinates the department's outreach efforts to local high schools and community colleges and teaches courses on interaction design and usability evaluation each academic year.
