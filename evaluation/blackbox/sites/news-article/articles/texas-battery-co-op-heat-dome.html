How a Texas Co-op's Battery Fleet Survived the August Heat Dome

Eleven straight days above 105°F tested a 90-megawatt battery fleet built for ordinary summers. It held, barely, with help from an old-fashioned tool: asking members to use less.

By Priya Chandran, Senior Energy Correspondent

Published August 24, 2026, 8:30 AM CT · Updated August 24, 2026, 4:10 PM CT · 5 min read

When the heat dome settled over central Texas on August 12, the region's electric cooperative had a plan built around four hours of battery discharge each evening. By day six, the plan was no longer enough on its own.

"Our batteries were doing exactly what they were designed to do," said the cooperative's chief operating officer in an interview. "The problem was that a plan built for a three-day event doesn't automatically scale to an eleven-day one." Overnight lows stayed above 82°F for most of the stretch, which meant the batteries recharged to only about 85 percent of capacity most nights instead of the usual full charge.

The fix was mostly human, not technical

Rather than draw on emergency gas contracts, the cooperative issued a voluntary conservation appeal to its roughly 45,000 residential members on days seven through eleven, asking them to pre-cool homes before 4 p.m. and delay laundry and dishwasher use until after 9 p.m. Member response, measured through smart-meter data, trimmed peak demand by an estimated 6 to 8 percent on the days the appeal was issued.

That margin, combined with the partially recharged batteries, was enough to avoid rotating outages. The cooperative's leadership was candid that the margin was thin. "Day nine was closer than we'd like to admit," the COO said.

For the broader trend behind this kind of battery-first planning, see the Ledger's companion piece: "Utilities Are Quietly Betting Summer Peaks on Batteries, Not Gas Peakers."

What the co-op is changing

The cooperative says it is now modeling multi-day heat events rather than single-day peaks when sizing future battery additions, and is evaluating a second battery site to add roughly 40 megawatts of capacity before next summer.
